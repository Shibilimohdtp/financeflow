Stillwater Bloom

A new visual direction for FinanceFlow, made by changing tokens and shared components rather than screens. It grows out of Lichen: an airy aurora canvas, big soft squircles, light oversized numerals, ink pills and one lime spark.

Direction

FinanceFlow should feel like a calm, well-lit room where your money sits in a few soft, solid objects. The page is a pale wash of lilac, blush and moss light. Money lives in large squircle cards; the few that matter most (an account, a loan, a goal) become grainy gradient “stones” cut from Lichen's own colours. Numbers are big and light, units are small and quiet. Everything you tap is ink. One lime spark marks the thing you are looking at right now. Everyday lists stay plain, dense and fast.

  • Light numbers, heavy meaning. Hierarchy comes from size and space, not bold weight.
  • Ink acts, lime points. Ink is every action and selection. Lime is a fill that marks the current value or a completed step, always with ink on it.
  • Spend effects where they mean something. Grain, gradient and glass only on hero surfaces and the dock. Lists and forms stay solid.
  • Two densities. Comfortable for summaries, compact for lists. Airy never means sparse.
  • Private by default, visibly. An “On device” cue on hero surfaces; amounts hide the same way everywhere.

Hero gradients (light, then dark)

Assigned by tone, not by brand: Moss = accounts and net worth, Rose = loans and debts, Dusk = investments and cards, Tide = savings goals, Sand = cash, wallets and subscriptions. Rose and Sand are pale with ink text in light mode; in dark mode every hero is deep with light text.

Contrast results

Money colours are the shared set already approved for DS-8 (expense moves to brick). Income against expense: . Lime is a fill only; lime against white is 1.3:1 so it never carries meaning alone, and selected states also change to ink.

Type

The same money line set in three open-licence (OFL) families at weight 300 with tabular figures. The checks under each card run in your browser: they test whether the font itself has the ₹ glyph and real tabular figures, rather than borrowing them from a fallback font.

Recommendation

Geist for UI and numerals, Manrope kept as the fallback, Fraunces dropped

Geist is a neo-grotesque with a crisp light weight; it reads closest to the reference and stays calm in lists. It has ₹ and tabular figures. We bundle 300, 400 and 500 as static cuts (the same make_static_fonts.py route as Manrope); Manrope stays as the fallback family. Inter (with its Display optical size) is the second choice: equally solid, but it is the face nearly every app uses, so it does less for our identity. Fraunces no longer fits the light, technical tone; onboarding and the loan hero move to the light display numerals.

Money display rule

  • FfAmountText gets a style: split (big number, small raised currency symbol, small light paise; for hero, lg and stat tiles) and inline (one size, tabular, sign first; for rows and tables). sm is always inline.
  • Split degrades: when the amount does not fit, it steps down hero → lg → md before ellipsis (FittedBox clamp at 0.72), text scale clamped at 1.3× as today.
  • Paise are hidden on hero values when they are zero, never in rows. Indian grouping (4,82,310) comes from the locale formatter, not the widget.

Components

New and changed shared widgets. Each takes tones and variants, no raw colours, and gets widget tests in every palette, both modes and at 2× text.

Floating dock and the five tabs

The dock replaces StillwaterBottomNavigationBar inside the same shell, with the same five destinations (Home, Money, Plan, Insights, More) and badges. The selected tab expands into an ink pill with its label; the others are 48 dp circles with tooltips and semantic labels. Pages get bottom padding from FfDock.insetOf(context). With reduced effects, or at 1.5× text and above, it falls back to a full-width solid bar with labels under every icon. The current widget stays as that fallback, so nothing is lost.

Options

Recommended

A. Evolve Lichen into Bloom

One new default. Lichen's colours become the gradients, the aurora and the focus ring; actions turn ink; lime is added. Tide and Graphite are then built in the same language (Tide: sea-glass aurora, tide/sand gradients, same lime; Graphite: neutral aurora, mono gradients, lime only on data). Fits the decision that Lichen stays the identity, refined. Risk: everyone sees the change at once, so it lands behind the gallery and screenshots first.

B. Separate “Bloom” preset

Today's look stays default; Bloom is a palette plus style switch in settings. Safest, but every component needs both shapes, densities and number styles maintained, which doubles test and screenshot work, and the old style keeps leaking.

C. Hybrid

New shape, type and components; palette stays Lichen with only lime added (no aurora, no gradients). Cheapest, but it loses most of what makes the reference feel alive.

A keeps a back door: FfShape.style and FfEffects can restore rounded corners and flat surfaces with one token each.

Risks

  • Performance. Blur and large gradients on mid-range Android. Mitigated by blur only on the dock and hero glass, grain baked once, no effects in list items, the reduced-effects mode, and frame timing on the device in DS-B5.
  • Startup. Grain generation and font registration must not delay the first frame; grain starts after the first route renders.
  • Parity test churn. DS-B1 intentionally changes ThemeData; the parity test moves from “identical” to an explicit list of intended differences.
  • Legibility of light weights at small sizes: weight 300 only at 28 dp and above.
  • Flutter upgrade (for native superellipse) is a build change of its own and is not part of this plan.

Questions for you

  1. Direction: A (evolve Lichen), B (separate preset) or C (hybrid)? I recommend A.
  2. Typeface: Geist (recommended), Inter, or Manrope at a new light weight? Downloading the chosen family's files from the Google Fonts GitHub repository needs your OK, as it did for Manrope.
  3. Money style: split style for hero, large and stat amounts, inline for rows? I recommend yes.
  4. Effects: glass, blur and grain on hero surfaces and the dock only? I recommend yes.
  5. Dock: replace the bottom bar with the floating dock (bar kept as fallback)? I recommend yes.
  6. Primary buttons turn ink (Lichen green moves to gradients, focus and selected tints)? I recommend yes.
  7. Balance: is about 75% resemblance to the reference right, or do you want it closer or further away?
